## Research Question

Earthquake-resistant design often relies on translational ground-motion descriptions, while emerging rotational sensors can record additional components of motion. This project asks what rotational ground motions imply for bridge structures and how they should be characterized for engineering interpretation.

## Method

The work combines rotational ground-motion characterization, structural-dynamics reasoning, and earthquake-engineering interpretation. Rather than treating rotations as a purely seismological signal, the project places them in the context of design-relevant structural response.

## Contribution

This case study shows Dr. Dhabu's interdisciplinary role clearly: the research translates between measured or simulated seismic motion and the practical questions asked by civil engineers. It makes the Work page stronger because it demonstrates application, not only theory.

## Outputs

- Collaborative arXiv preprint on rotational ground motions and earthquake-resistant bridge design.
- Research bridge between 6C sensing, seismological characterization, and structural response.
- Foundation for future work on how rotational components can inform infrastructure resilience.

## Why It Matters

Bridges and other critical structures can be sensitive to complex motion. When rotational measurements are interpreted carefully, they can add information that helps engineers understand loading, response, and design assumptions more completely.
