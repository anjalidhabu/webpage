## Project Summary
This research develops a generalized framework for seismic wave propagation in layered reduced micropolar media, enabling simultaneous simulation of translational and rotational earthquake ground motions. By extending classical elastic formulations to incorporate micro-rotational effects, the work advances the theoretical and computational foundations of rotational seismology and next-generation earthquake ground-motion modeling.

The study combines analytical wave-propagation theory, layered-medium recursion, Green’s-function formulation, and numerical simulation within a unified framework capable of modeling complex seismic behavior in horizontally layered Earth structures. The formulation was validated against limiting classical elastic cases and applied to the 2012 Mw 5.9 Wutai, Taiwan earthquake.
## Research Problem

Rotational ground motions are increasingly measurable, but conventional elastic models can underestimate them because they represent the Earth medium primarily through translation-focused continuum mechanics. This work explored reduced micropolar theory as an alternative medium model that can represent micro-rotational effects while remaining simpler than full micropolar elasticity.

The central research question was whether a layered reduced micropolar representation of the Earth medium could simulate translational and rotational ground motions more realistically than a classical elastic layered half-space, especially for near-field rotations.

## Methods and Technical Expertise

- **Reduced Micropolar Continuum Modeling:** Formulated layered Earth media using reduced micropolar material behavior with translations and rotations represented together.
- **Analytical Wave Propagation:** Derived Green's functions for layered reduced micropolar half-space response under earthquake loading.
- **P-SV and SH Wave Decomposition:** Separated the coupled wave-propagation problem into independent P-SV and SH systems for tractable computation.
- **Layered-Medium Recursion:** Used generalized reflection and transmission coefficients to propagate wavefields through multiple horizontal layers.
- **Numerical Simulation:** Evaluated frequency-wavenumber integrals to compute surface translations and rotations in the time-space domain.
- **Validation and Benchmarking:** Checked limiting cases against classical elastic layered media and compared simulated motions for the 2012 Wutai earthquake.
- **Research Programming:** Implemented computational routines for simulated translational velocities, rotational rates, and source-to-site response analysis.

## Research Contributions

- **Layered reduced micropolar Earth model:** Built a theoretical representation of the Earth as a horizontally layered reduced micropolar half-space, with each layer described through elastic, density, rotational inertia, and additional reduced micropolar material parameters.
- **Green's-function formulation:** Derived analytical Green's functions for the response of layered reduced micropolar media subjected to earthquake force, enabling surface translations and rotations to be computed from the same framework.
- **Wave-propagation solver structure:** Reduced the propagation problem into P-SV and SH components and used reflection/transmission recursions at layer interfaces to model energy transfer through layered media.
- **Classical-elastic validation workflow:** Tested the formulation by choosing reduced micropolar parameters that converge toward the classical elastic case, then comparing simulated translational and rotational responses.
- **Earthquake simulation case study:** Simulated ground motions for the 2012 Mw 5.9 Wutai, Taiwan earthquake and compared reduced micropolar results with classical elastic layered-medium simulations.
- **Reusable simulation codebase:** Produced code for generating simulated ground motions, later made available through the paper's data repository.

## Key Findings

- Translational velocities from the layered reduced micropolar model remained close to those from the layered classical elastic model.
- Rotational rates were substantially more sensitive to the reduced micropolar material representation than translational motions.
- For magnitude-distance simulations, rotational rates from the reduced micropolar half-space were higher than those from the classical elastic half-space.
- For the 2012 Wutai earthquake, the simulated vertical-axis peak rotational motion agreed well with the value reported in the literature.
- The results indicate that reduced micropolar theory can help explain large rotational amplitudes observed in near-field earthquake records.

## My Role

I led the formal analysis, numerical implementation, validation, visualization, and original manuscript preparation for this study. ## Challenges and Lessons Learned

A central challenge was making a richer continuum model computationally usable for earthquake ground-motion simulation. Reduced micropolar theory introduces additional material parameters and rotational degrees of freedom, but experimental values for those parameters are still limited. The project therefore required a careful validation strategy: first showing convergence to classical elasticity under limiting parameter choices, then exploring how rotations respond when reduced micropolar effects are retained.

Another challenge was keeping the formulation general enough for arbitrary earthquake loading while still practical for numerical evaluation. Working in the frequency-wavenumber domain made the analytical expressions compact, but the final surface translations and rotations required stable numerical integration over wavenumber and frequency.

## Outcome and Research Continuity

The work established a layered reduced micropolar framework for simulating translational and rotational earthquake ground motions. It showed that rotations are more sensitive than translations to reduced micropolar material behavior, and that the model can better capture large rotational amplitudes reported in near-field earthquake observations.

The project became a theoretical foundation for later research on rotational seismology, heterogeneous media, 6C ground-motion simulation, and structural interpretation of rotations and strains.

## Data and Code Availability

No previously published dataset was used in this study. The simulation codes developed for the paper were made available through Mendeley Data and can be used to reproduce the simulated ground-motion examples reported in the article.
