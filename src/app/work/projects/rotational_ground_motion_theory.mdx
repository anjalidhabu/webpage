## Research Question

Classical elastic models are often built around translational motion, but rotational effects and strain can be important for earthquake ground-motion interpretation. This work asks whether reduced micropolar theory can provide a useful framework for modeling Earth media and simulating translational and rotational ground motions together.

## Method

The research developed mathematical formulations for reduced micropolar half-spaces and layered media. It included fundamental solutions for static and dynamic loading, simulation of translational and rotational ground motion, and wave-propagation studies that connect theory with earthquake-source and medium-response questions.

## Contribution

This body of work established a theoretical foundation that carries into Dr. Dhabu's current research. It shows the analytical depth behind the later simulation and structural-response projects: rotations are treated as part of the physical model, not as an afterthought added after translation-only analysis.

## Outputs

- Journal article on seismic wave propagation through layered reduced micropolar media in JGR: Solid Earth.
- Journal article on translational and rotational ground-motion simulation in homogeneous reduced micropolar half-space.
- Journal article on fundamental solutions for homogeneous reduced micropolar half-space.
- Conference contribution asking whether reduced micropolar theory can model Earth media and simulate earthquake ground motions.

## Why It Matters

The theory gives a bridge between seismology and earthquake engineering. It supports richer descriptions of ground motion and helps explain when rotational components and strains may matter for structures, sensors, and simulation workflows.
