## Project Summary

This project used extreme value theory to characterize the parts of earthquake slip models that are most responsible for strong motion generation. The work treated high-slip regions as statistical extremes, then used threshold-based generalized Pareto modeling to identify strong-motion generation regions in finite-source rupture models.

The study analyzed 159 filtered slip models from the SRCMOD finite-source rupture database. It first defined effective rupture dimensions so that insignificant edge regions in source-inversion models were removed, then identified the threshold slip above which the remaining extreme-slip values follow a generalized Pareto distribution.

## Research Problem

Finite-source inversion models often contain large rectangular fault planes with low-slip or zero-slip regions near the edges. These regions can inflate rupture dimensions and blur the interpretation of which parts of the fault actually control damaging ground motion.

Earlier definitions of asperity or large-slip regions were often empirical and commonly assumed that the full slip distribution could be treated with a single probability model. This project asked whether the strong-motion generation region could be defined more rigorously by using extreme value theory on the high-slip tail of each source model.

## Method

The workflow combined rupture trimming, statistical threshold selection, and regression analysis:

- **Effective slip dimensions:** Developed a strong-motion-duration-based trimming method to remove insignificant edge rows and columns while conserving the source regions that dominate the rupture process.
- **Extreme-value thresholding:** Used Zipf plots, mean excess function plots, and moment-ratio plots as a three-step procedure to identify a threshold slip for each model.
- **Generalized Pareto modeling:** Defined the strong-motion generation region as the part of the effective slip model where slip exceeds the threshold and follows a generalized Pareto distribution.
- **Source-scaling relationships:** Estimated effective length, effective width, effective area, effective mean slip, threshold slip, and area of strong-motion generation as functions of seismic moment.
- **Comparison with prior methods:** Compared the proposed source-scaling relations with approaches from Somerville et al., Mai and Beroza, and Mai et al.

## Research Contributions

- Proposed an extreme-value-theory definition of strong-motion generation regions in earthquake slip models.
- Introduced an effective rupture-dimension workflow based on strong motion duration and cumulative slip contribution.
- Applied the method to 159 SRCMOD slip models spanning reverse, strike-slip, and normal fault mechanisms.
- Derived source-scaling equations for rupture dimensions, mean effective slip, threshold slip, and strong-motion generation area.
- Showed that the high-slip tail is better treated separately from the main slip distribution, supporting a two-part statistical interpretation of earthquake slip.

## Key Findings

- Most effective slip models broadly follow an exponential distribution, but the largest slip values deviate from that distribution.
- The largest slip values can be modeled as extremes using the generalized Pareto distribution after a suitable threshold is chosen.
- Regions with slip above the threshold provide a statistically grounded definition of strong-motion generation regions.
- Effective rupture dimensions and strong-motion generation areas increase approximately linearly with seismic moment on a logarithmic scale.
- For a given seismic moment, the proposed area of strong-motion generation is smaller than asperity or large-slip areas estimated by several earlier empirical definitions.

## Outcome and Research Continuity

The project provided a statistical route for defining strong-motion generation regions in earthquake source models. It also produced source-scaling relationships that can be used to estimate rupture parameters for future events when seismic moment is known.

This work broadened the research portfolio from wave propagation and rotational ground-motion modeling into earthquake source processes. It supports the larger research direction of understanding how source behavior, propagation effects, and near-surface conditions jointly control damaging ground motions.
